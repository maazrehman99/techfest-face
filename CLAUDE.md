## 1. What we are building and why
A **face recognition system tuned for Pakistani faces**, for the competition **NED University CSIT Tech Fest '26, "Nemo's Deep Dive", Round 2 "Every Fish Has a Face"** (AI Alliance). The team (three friends) is judged on pipeline quality, fine-tuning on Pakistani data, evaluation rigor, and the presentation. Dates: **report due 2026-10-22; the organizers run a hidden benchmark on 2026-10-23.**

The user's own goal: the model must work well on real Pakistani faces including **dupatta, scarf/hijab, turban, cap, beard, masks, CCTV/low-res/low-light**. Two modes, one model:
1. **Verify** (what the organizers test): two images in, "same person / different person" plus a similarity score out. Entry point `run.py` (`verify`, `embed`, `pairs`). Offline, CPU, never crashes on a face-less image.
2. **Face memory** (the user's own enhancement): enroll a person once with consent, then on images/video/live camera show the person's **name + match score, or "Unknown"**. No retraining per person: the model is frozen, a person is remembered as stored embeddings. Files `gallery.py` (CLI) and `live.py` (browser UI on port 8000).

## 2. The pipeline (all in `pipeline.py`)
detect (SCRFD-10G, 5 landmarks) -> quality filter (blur, brightness, size, detector score) -> **align to 112x112** -> embed (ArcFace ResNet-50 `w600k_r50`, 512-d) -> cosine similarity -> threshold.
Reused, not written by us: InsightFace `buffalo_l` models in `models/`. **Weights are non-commercial research only** (fine for the competition; cite them in the report; never present as a product).
`FACE_PROVIDER=cuda` forces GPU and raises an error instead of silently falling back to CPU. `FACE_WORKERS`, `FACE_THREADS` control parallelism.

## 3. Decisions already made (do not re-litigate; full reasoning in `DECISIONS.md`)
- Reuse InsightFace ArcFace R50 as baseline; AdaFace was the optional challenger (not pursued). Fine-tune only if it beats the baseline on validation.
- Thresholds are chosen on a validation split, never on test. Verify threshold in `config.json` = 0.276 (val FAR 1%). `identify_threshold` 0.40 for the open-set gallery is a conservative PLACEHOLDER, still to be tuned.
- **Data (locked 2026-10-20):** public South Asian data + CC-licensed/public-domain photos of **Pakistani public figures from Wikimedia**. **No LLM/AI-generated faces for identity training** (inconsistent identities, stereotypes, domain gap); at most as labelled illustrations in slides. The team does not collect large private data; the three team members may enroll themselves with written consent as the demo gallery only (far too few for training/metrics).
- Coverings (dupatta, hijab, turban, cap, beard...) come from synthetic overlays (`augment.py`, applied to TRAINING crops only, gender-aware: beard only on male faces) plus the real public-figure photos. The overlays are crude flat shapes; the model barely reacts to dupatta/turban/cap overlays, and reacts strongly to masks, cloth covers, beards and CCTV degradation (see section 5).
- Compute: user has no GPU. Free Colab T4, or a teammate's RTX 5080 PC. Colab TPUs are unusable (code needs NVIDIA).
- Honesty is a requirement: the report must state that halfFace is a South Asian proxy (not Pakistani-specific), that Wikimedia images are public figures from article pages (celebrity-style), and that the ArcFace-based outlier cleaning is lenient (0.15) on purpose.

Wikimedia notes: `commons.wikimedia.org` is unreachable from the user's network; the collector works via Wikidata + Wikipedia article images in 20 languages + upload.wikimedia.org. Wikidata "citizen of Pakistan" wrongly includes East-Pakistan-era Bangladeshis, so the query excludes anyone also Bangladeshi and anyone born outside Pakistan, born >= 1947. **Label noise (found 2026-10-21 by eyeballing samples):** Wikipedia article images often show other people (e.g. Ahsan Iqbal's page had Nawaz Sharif and Shahid Khaqan Abbasi photos), group photos, medals, graves, crowds. `process_wikimedia.py` therefore keeps only dominant-face images, trusts name-in-filename 'anchor' images, and admits other images only if they match the anchors (cosine >= 0.40); people without an anchor are dropped. Always eyeball a sample of the final crops per split. **Actual yield (2026-10-21): 1500 people checked -> 688 free images -> 56 people with >=3 -> after face/dominance/anchor cleaning 13 people, 48 images (train 10 people/35 imgs, val 2/6, test 1/7; 11 male, 2 female).** That is far too small to fine-tune on or to compute EER on (the test split has one person, so no impostor pairs). Cleaned crops look correct on inspection (`results/pk_wiki/contact_*.jpg`), apart from one probable mislabel (Hamid Mir, 2nd image) and a few near-duplicate photos. Original estimate was 'hundreds of images', so fine-tuning is deliberately conservative and the Wikimedia test split is the main Pakistani proof.

## 5. Results so far
- **Baseline (frozen ArcFace R50) on halfFace test, 3312 pairs:** EER 2.32%, AUC 0.993, accuracy 97.6%, precision 0.979, recall 0.973, F1 0.976, TAR@FAR1% 95.8%, TAR@FAR0.1% 88.2%, no-face rate 1.8%. Val thresholds: 0.261 (EER), 0.276 (FAR 1%).
- Unmasked accuracy 99.85%; masked 96.6% (FAR 4.6%). Weaker: right-facing poses (95.3%), sharp images (96.4%). **Ablation:** without alignment EER jumps to 27.6% (alignment is essential); quality filter ~no effect here; flip averaging helps slightly (EER 2.11%).
- Caveats: halfFace is controlled/cooperative, so there is little headroom; test split is male-heavy (258 of 3312 pairs female). That is why the Pakistani held-out split matters.
- **Augmentation effect** (60 people, mean same-person cosine; clean 0.728): cloth cover 0.417, surgical mask 0.458, CCTV 0.561, beard 0.572, hijab 0.611, blur 0.625, dupatta 0.668, turban 0.680, cap 0.683. See `results/aug/`.
- Face-memory small test (halfFace, 15 people, 3 enroll photos each): 33/37 masked probes correct, 4 Unknown, 0 wrong. Unknown-person rejection not yet measured.

## 8. Rules for working here
- **Never train on halfFace or on the `test` split**; never edit the `split` column; never augment val/test. Choose configs by val only; limit tuning runs.
- Report negative results truthfully (e.g. "no epoch beat the baseline").
- Real people's faces: keep data local/private, do not upload or share it, record consent for anyone enrolled in a gallery (`gallery.py enroll` requires `--consent`), delete personal galleries from shared machines.
- The user prefers plain, simple language and wants the reason behind each step. Confirm before destructive or outward-facing actions (pushing to GitHub, sending requests, deleting data).
- Gotchas: `pkill -f <name>` can kill your own shell if the pattern appears in your command line (kill by PID instead); sklearn's LFW pixels are 0-1 floats; one ONNX process with all threads is ~10x slower than several processes with 2 threads each; plain `onnxruntime` shadows `onnxruntime-gpu` (uninstall it); new PyTorch `torch.onnx.export` needs `dynamo=False` unless `onnxscript` is installed; the RTX 5080 (Blackwell) needs driver >= 570 and PyTorch built for CUDA 12.8 (`cu128`).
- **Windows 11 target:** the teammates run `finetune.py` on Windows; the project was only ever run on Linux. Windows traps: OpenCV can't read images from non-ASCII folder paths, always pass `encoding="utf-8"` to `open()` (names are Urdu/Unicode), DataLoader workers use spawn (try `--workers 0` if it hangs), `insightface` needs a C++ compiler (avoid it for fine-tuning), PowerShell syntax for env vars (`$env:FACE_PROVIDER="cuda"`), venv activation needs `Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process Bypass`, Python 3.11/3.12 only. `models/` is git-ignored (167 MB ONNX): download steps in `HANDOFF_FINETUNE.md`.
